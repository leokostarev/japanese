In Japanese, there are two kinds of adjectives: い-adjectives and な-adjectives.
They are named for their last character when they attach to nouns.

> ちいさい ほん

First, let’s take a look at this phrase.
The word ちいさい is an い-adjective, meaning ‘small’.
ほん is ’book’, so this phrase means ‘a small book’.

> おおきい ほん

We could also have a big book.

> かわいい いぬ

And we’ve got a cute dog.
Notice that the word order is the same as in English - the adjective comes before the noun.

> しずかな ひと

It works the same way for a な-adjective. Look at the phrase above.
しずか（な）means ‘quiet’ (notice the な is often written in parentheses in a dictionary; more on that later).
ひと is ‘person’, so this phrase means ‘a quiet person’.

> げんきな がくせい

We could also have an energetic student.

> きれいな へや

Or we could have a pretty room.

> じしょ は おおきい ほん です。

We can add these phrases into our AはBです pattern that we already learned.
The above reads: a dictionary (じしょ) is a big book (おおきい ほん).

> かのじょ は しずかな ひと です。

We can add these phrases into our AはBです pattern that we already learned.
She (かのじょ) is a quiet person (しずかな ひと).

> じしょ は おおきい です。

You could also drop the noun and just place an adjective before です。
This would read as: (the) dictionary is big.

> かのじょ は しずか です。

 She is quiet.
Notice that in this pattern, AはAdjectiveです, you do not include the な in the な-adjective.
It is only used when modifying a noun. (しずか な かのじょ)
This is why some dictionaries will write it as しずか（な）.

It helps show that it is an な-adjective, but lets you know that you don't always need it. (Only if it's before a noun!)
