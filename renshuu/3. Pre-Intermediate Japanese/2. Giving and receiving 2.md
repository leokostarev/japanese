In the last lesson, we talked about giving and receiving objects.
But did you know that you can 'give' and 'receive' actions in Japanese, too?

> て上げる  
> てくれる  
> てもらう

Using the same three verbs for giving and receiving, あげる、くれる、and もらう、you can talk about doing and receiving favors.
Instead of (Object)を and the verb, you will instead use the て form of the verb showing the action being done as a favor.
You will use the same rules to create the て form of the verb as you have learned before, so make sure you know if you're using an Ichidan, Godan, or irregular verb!

> 読んで 上げる  
> 料理をして くれる  
> 教えて もらう

You will also need to consider the rules from the last lesson about when to use あげる vs くれる.

>上げる ->
> ((You) Your family/friends) Everyone else
> <- くれる

So remember: you use くれる when something is given to you or someone in your circle, and あげる otherwise.

> おじいさん に 本 を 読んで あげます。  
> お母さん は 私たち に 料理をして くれた。  
> 学生 は 先生 に 言葉 の 意味 を 教えて もらいました。

When you see sentences like these in English, it will often translate the same whether or not you have the てあげる (etc) in there or not: the 'favor' is implied.
So the first sentence would translate as “I read a book to my grandfather (as a favor).”
The second sentence would be “My mom cooked for us (as a favor).”
The third sentence could be translated as “The students (received a favor and) were taught the meaning of the word by the teacher.”
As you can see, it can sometimes be awkward to translate sentences like these into English, because doing and receiving favors is often implied.
In Japanese, performing and receiving favors is an important part of the culture, so sentences like these are much more common!

Give it a try, and do something nice for someone!
