> もらう

Here, we'll introduce three new verbs: あげる、くれる、and もらう.
あげる and くれる are both used to talk about giving, and もらう means 'to receive'.
Let's start with もらう, since it is more straightforward!

> 手紙 を もらいました。  
> お母さん から お金 を もらう。  
> 兄 は 先生 に 本 を もらった。

When you receive something, the most basic way to say it is (object)をもらう, as you see in the first sentence: “I received a letter.”
If you want to mention who you received something from, you can do it in one of two ways:
One way would be to use the word から (from) that we have already learned.
So the second sentence here would read, “I receive money from my mother.”
The other way, shown in the third sentence, would be to use particle に - yes, another way to use this particle!
Particle に marks an 'indirect object,' so it shows who or what the action is coming from (or in other words, the giver).
So, the third sentence would say “My brother received a book from his teacher.”

> (Recipient) は (Giver) に/から (Object) を もらう

Either way you choose to say 'from,' the pattern will still be the same:

> 先生 は 私 に 本 を もらった。 (weird)

One thing to note with もらう is that it assumes you are identifying with the recipient, so it would be weird to use it when you are the giver!

> 上げる  
> くれる

The verbs あげる and くれる both mean 'to give,' and grammatically will work much in the same way as もらう.
However, when to use each verb depends on who is giving to whom.

> ((You) Your family/friends) Everyone else
> 上げる ->

Picture two circles, one inside the other: you are in the inner circle, your friends and family are in the outer circle, and everyone else is outside both circles.
If the gift is going from the middle towards the outside (eg from you to a friend or family member, or from a friend/family member to someone else), you will use あげる.

> 友達 に クッキー を あげる。  
> お父さん は 医者 に ペン を あげました。  
> 私 は あの 人 に 傘 を あげた。

So whether you give your friend cookies, or your dad gave the doctor a pen, or you gave that person an umbrella, the object moves away from you, so you will use あげる.

> 先生 は 学生 に 紙 を あげます。  
> 姉 は 友達 に 花 を あげた。

You would also use あげる if someone outside the circle is giving something to someone else outside the circle, or if a friend/family member gave something to another friend/family member.
So if the teacher gives the student paper, or if your sister gave her friend a flower, you will still use あげる.

> ((You) Your family/friends) Everyone else
> <- くれる

If, instead, the object is going from the outside inward (eg from someone unknown to a friend, or from a family member to you), then you will use くれる.

> 友達 は 私 に クッキー を くれる。  
> 医者 は お父さん に ペン を くれました。  
> あの 人 は 傘 を くれた。

So if your friend gives you cookies, or the doctor gave your dad the pen, or if that person gave you an umbrella, the object moves inwards on the circle, so you will use くれる.

> (Giver) は (Recipient) に (Object) を 上げる/くれる

Notice that with あげる and くれる, the word order and particles are flipped from what you use with もらう.
Here, the giver is the subject and the receiver is the indirect object!
So you'll use (Giver) は (Recipient) に (Object) をあげる/くれる.
You cannot use から interchangeably with に here, because you are giving TO someone, rather than receiving FROM them.

Giving and receiving are important parts of Japanese culture: now you can talk about them in Japanese, too!
